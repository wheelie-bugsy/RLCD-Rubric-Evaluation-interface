Last Tuesday I was responsible for sending the quarterly report to our client, Brightwell, by 5pm. At 3pm I discovered that the sales figures from the regional team were missing, and I spent two hours chasing them instead of telling my manager. The report went out at 7pm and the client complained to our account director.

I felt panicked and embarrassed. Looking back, I think I avoided escalating because I didn't want to look like I couldn't cope, and that pride made the delay worse. My manager told me afterwards she could have called the regional lead directly and fixed it in ten minutes.

The real cause was not the missing data but that I had no checkpoint: I only checked inputs on the deadline day. I also assumed asking for help was a weakness, when in our team escalation is expected. This links to what we covered on "managing up" - surfacing risks early is part of the job.

Next quarter I will set a data-collection deadline three days before the report is due, put it in the shared calendar, and if anything is missing 24 hours before I will message my manager straight away. I'll judge success by whether the report goes out on time with no last-minute chasing.
